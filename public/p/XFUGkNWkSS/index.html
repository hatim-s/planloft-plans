Hub Siting: app brief and glossary

Hub Siting is a rebuild of Advance Auto Parts' internal "Hub & Market Hub Siting" tool. Network and real-estate planners use it to decide where AAP should open its next Hubs and Market Hubs. It is one full-screen map workspace that ranks candidate sites by the business case for each one.

The business problem

AAP stocks its stores through a tiered network: a distribution centre (DC) feeds Market Hubs, Market Hubs feed Hubs, and Hubs refill nearby stores during the day. The customers that matter most here are Pro Customers, the repair shops and garages that buy parts commercially and need them fast. So the tool measures everything by what a site can reach in a 30-minute drive, modelled on Tuesday 10:30 traffic.

A good site reaches many Pro Customers, supports stores that have no hub today, sits where AAP's share against AutoZone, O'Reilly and NAPA is low, and serves stores with high sales.

Suggested sites are always new locations, never an existing store. Each one is tied to an anchor store or hub nearby.

How it decides

The app pulls six read-only data feeds from Snowflake: suggested sites, AAP's own locations, pending Market Hubs, competitor stores, Pro Customer cells, and the stores linked to a selected site. A model running in the browser then works through every site:

  1. It sets the area type (urban, suburban or rural) from household density and an adjustable urban cut-off.
  2. It weights the Pro Customer reach by area type.
  3. It assigns a sales tier from the average rolling 12-month sales of the stores the site would support.
  4. It checks the planner's rules and records each failure in plain English.
  5. It scores each site 0 to 100 within its tier, using weighted percentile ranks of four inputs: aligned customers 35, reach 25, competitor gap 20, support gap 20.
  6. It keeps one site per area. A site within 8 miles of a higher-ranked site becomes that site's alternate instead of getting its own rank.

Every control re-runs the model straight away, so planners can test "what if" scenarios without waiting on the server.

What is on screen

The header holds the title, the method summary and three map layer switches: Pro Customers, Competitor stores and Our locations.

The left panel is where planners set the scenario:

  • State search, with a count of suggested sites per state. The selection is kept in the URL so a view can be shared.
  • Tier cards: Tier 1 ($1.7M+), Tier 2 ($1.5M to $1.7M), Tier 3 (under $1.5M), each split into Market Hubs and Hubs.
  • The facility switch (All, Market Hubs, Hubs) and a "Show excluded" checkbox.
  • Competitive Position, Level 1 to 4, which caps AAP's share of stores in the area.
  • The density modifier: urban cut-off, customer weights per area type, and which area types may host each facility.
  • Rules and weights: minimum customer counts, minimum stores without hub support, and the four ranking weights.
  • The ranked list of suggested sites, grouped by tier.

The map shows recommended sites coloured by facility and ringed by tier, AAP's existing network, pending Market Hubs, Pro Customer density and competitor stores. Selecting a site draws its anchor store and the stores it would support.

The scorecard opens for the selected site. It shows the rank, tier, area type and score, a five-point "Why this site", any rules the site fails, and a table of reach, competitors, AAP's share, hub support and the stores the site would support.

Known gaps in the data

The tool says plainly where the data falls short, and these gaps shape the results:

  • There are no drive-time areas, so "one site per area" uses an 8-mile straight-line radius.
  • Aligned Pro Customers is empty, so its weight goes to a precomputed composite score whose formula is not documented.
  • The under-supported hub count and the maximum stores per Hub are missing, so those two rules are disabled.
  • Hub support is 85 to 100% everywhere, so the "already hub-supported" rule is off by default.
  • The data is development data, not production.

Glossary

Term Meaning
AAP Advance Auto Parts, the retailer that owns the tool
Carquest AAP's second store brand. It counts as "ours"
Store A regular AAP retail location
Hub A larger location that keeps more stock and refills nearby stores
Market Hub The biggest stocking location below a DC, serving a wider area
DC Distribution centre, the top of the supply chain
Pending Market Hub A planned Market Hub. The model treats it as already open
Pro Customer A commercial buyer such as a repair shop or garage
R12 net sales Net sales over the rolling last 12 months
Suggested site A candidate spot for a new Hub or Market Hub, never an existing location
Anchor store The existing AAP location a suggested site is tied to
Supported stores Stores within 30 minutes the new site would serve
Hub-supported A store with a Hub or Market Hub within 30 minutes
Store without hub support A store with no hub within 30 minutes. This is the support gap
Tier 1 / 2 / 3 Average R12 sales of the supported stores: $1.7M+, $1.5M to $1.7M, under $1.5M
30-minute reach Pro Customers within a 30-minute drive
Weighted reach Reach after applying the weight for each area type
Urban cut-off Households per square mile at or above which a site counts as urban (default 2,213)
Density modifier The cut-off, customer weights and allowed area types per facility
Our share AAP and Carquest stores divided by all auto-parts stores within 30 minutes
Competitive Position Cap on our share. Level 1 under 19.6%, Level 2 under 25%, Level 3 under 32%, Level 4 no cap
Competitor gap 1 minus our share. Higher means more room to grow
Support gap The number of stores without hub support near a site
Aligned Pro Customers Customers already tied to nearby stores. Empty in this dataset
Score 0 to 100, the weighted percentile rank of a site within its tier
Alternate A qualifying site within 8 miles of a higher-ranked one
Excluded A site that fails a rule or that the data marks as excluded
AutoZone, O'Reilly, NAPA The three competitor brands the tool tracks

The tool gives planners a ranked, explainable shortlist of where the next Hub or Market Hub should go. Each pick comes with the numbers that justify it.
