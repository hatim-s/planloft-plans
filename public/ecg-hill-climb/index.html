What I changed, in commit 4a89197b:

  • F1. The pinned card lists every rule. A rule that reaches more than three types opens into chips. The card scrolls inside the canvas and stays clear of the controls.
  • F2. The node list shows record counts, sorts by most records, and has a "No records" filter.
  • F3. The note says "Opened Regression + 5 similar to show Bug". Every chip in a pinned card goes to what it names, and the card then offers "Back to …".
  • F4. The level label opens a menu for Groups, Types, All types and Rule names. Hovering a band at Groups lights it and its two groups.
  • F5. A key at the top left says what solid and dashed lines mean.
  • F6. A type's card reads its rules from the export's relationships, so Opportunity names OEP and UCB.
  • F7. Searching lets go of a highlighted group. Reset clears the view, pin, trail, search, filters and highlight.
  • F8. The complete, scrolling card covers it. I chose not to build a neighbours-only view.

Gemini corrected Sol on six points:

  • Bug's card does open once the zoom lands. Sol had looked during the glide.
  • The 49 between Employee and Feature shows on hover, not on click.
  • The unnamed dots in Sol's screenshot came from a frame taken before labels settled. On a settled view, Commit names 4 of its 5 types.
  • Keeping Unconnected off the canvas, leaving out names that would overlap, and zooming in until a merged group opens are earlier decisions. Gemini upheld all three.
  • The stuck dimming wasn't a filter. Hovering a group row lit it, and typing a search removed the row before the pointer could leave it, so nothing turned the highlight off.
  • Record ranking belongs mostly to the Nodes tab, so F2 dropped to minor.

My own checks caught two problems with the first card fix. Capping the card at the canvas height pushed it up into the page header, because the popover library keeps a card inside the window, not the canvas. Sliding it back inside the canvas then put it over Reset. The placement scoring had rated covering the controls as cheaper than covering Account's many links. Covering the controls now costs more than covering any amount of links, and the card is capped to the band between the top bar and the controls.

Learnings.

  • Copy that promises behaviour is a test. "Click to pin it for every rule" above a card showing five rules cost a major finding.
  • Merging belongs to the canvas. Cards are where people read detail, so they name the real types even inside a merged group.
  • Anything shaped like a button gets clicked. The level label looked like one and did nothing, so the auditor tried it first.
  • A highlight that only turns off when the pointer leaves an element gets stuck if that element is removed.
  • The checker paid for itself. It corrected six claims and stopped three deliberate choices from being undone.

What I changed, in commit 5b41731b:

  • F1. When no spot beside its node fits, which happens once the node is panned off, the card docks in the top-right corner and offers "Show on canvas".
  • F2. The cause was the data. The lab's derive script kept only the first 12 names per type. The export now has every name from the production capture: Pull Request 23, Release and Release (2) 13 each. The derive script no longer cuts them.
  • F3. Clicking a group narrows the list to its types ("19 in Account"), keeps it lit on the canvas, and adds a chip to let it go.
  • F4. The card is rebuilt for each type, so it starts at the top with every rule closed.

From this round, Gemini reproduced each finding with a Playwright script and reported exact positions and zoom values. That made the fixes quicker than round 1's code-only verdicts.

Learnings.

  • A placement fix has to hold in every state the card can be in. I tested the card when it opened, not after a pan.
  • When the UI contradicts itself, check the data pipeline too. The 12 came from a slice in the derive script, two steps before the UI.
  • A row that shows as selected has to change something. The group row looked picked and the list ignored it.
  • Rebuilding a component per item is the simplest way to reset its scroll position and open rows.

What I changed, in commit 2625396f:

  • F1. Rule names started at a fixed 2.2× zoom. On the smaller canvas the last merged group only opens at 2.27×, so All types never existed. Rule names now start at whichever is later: 2.2×, or 1.2 times the zoom where the last merged group opens. That is 2.24× at 1440 × 900 and 2.73× at 1280 × 800.
  • F2. The count follows the search and the picked group: Contact 6, Commit 0, back to 42 when the group is cleared.

Learnings.

  • A fixed zoom threshold breaks on smaller screens when the layout scales with the canvas. Work it out from the same geometry.
  • A count on a filter has to follow the scope that filter works in.
  • The climb levelled off: 8 findings, then 4, then 2, with none major in round 3. UI stayed at 7 in every round. A task-based audit finds problems with getting things done, not with how the page looks. Improving the look would need a round with its own brief.

Across the rounds

  • Gemini never reached Hatim's Chrome. Its browser tool returned "Browser use requires a trusted Node REPL browser service". Round 1 verdicts came from the code and the screenshots alone. Rounds 2 and 3 used Playwright, which was more precise.
  • My Chrome extension showed an error page for the local dev server, so I checked local builds with Playwright and the deployed preview in Chrome.
  • I showed Sol the earlier scores to keep its scale steady. That may also have anchored it. The task counts are the firmer measure: 5, then 7, then all 9 done.
  • After each round I reran every earlier round's Playwright check. None found a regression, and each run took about a minute.
  • Every fix went into the prototype's docs/decisions.md, so the next auditor's "kept on purpose" list came straight from it.
