What the numbers say

Successful builds cost more and take longer than terminal refusals: 173.2 seconds and $0.06068 on average, compared with 72.2 seconds and $0.04023 for the five briefs that never built. That gap is expected because successful cases progress through schema inspection, validation, save, and deployment.

Retries added 1,603 seconds and $0.58656 beyond the canonical 20-case view. Most of that recovery work sits in N11, N16, N19, and N20. N16 and N20 were the important confirmation: both moved from code-node parameter rejection to deployed, independently tested workflows after the normal input-schema flow began resolving DynamicSchemaField schemas.

No new test regression appeared after the resolver change. The four red TEST rows are either exact pre-fix reproductions or fixture-limited. N14 is the clearest authored-workflow bug and should be repaired. N03 also needs a workflow correction. N09 needs connector-level investigation, while N12 needs a real purchase-order fixture before assigning blame to the workflow.

Measurement notes

  • Generation time is wall time from the agent SSE invocation to its final response.
  • Cost is the exact session cost reported by the tenant observability record joined by caseId.
  • Built means a real workflow ID was returned and fetched successfully.
  • Deployed means the fetched definition reported deploymentState.status = DEPLOYED.
  • TEST means the platform runner reached COMPLETED, even when the automation intentionally returned success: false for a guarded business outcome.
  • The regression pass read back all 15 built workflows as deployed before execution.

The suite now has a reliable baseline: 15 deployed, 11 execution-clean, two recovered by the core schema fix, and four remaining failures with named owners.
